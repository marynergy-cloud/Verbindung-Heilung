The Manifesto

Connection & Healing. Education & Growth.

Summary

The manifesto describes MaryQi as a life philosophy: the lived unity of body, mind, and heart. It is divided into three chapters – connection and healing (in the sense of holism), education and growth, as well as practice and manifestation – and outlines concrete routines such as conscious breathing, qigong, fasting, and offline periods.

Philosophical Foundations

Foreword

Every person has their own nature. Follow it.

The MaryQi Philosophy

MaryQi is more than a method. It is a life philosophy. It stands for the lived unity of body, mind, and heart. We live truthfulness, meet the external world with respect, openness, and tolerance, yet remain firmly anchored in our own values.

The core is: connection, healing, growth. This does not arise through theory, but through lived practice and responsibility in the midst of everyday life. Through continuous mindfulness, we create the path to inner freedom.

The goal is holistic health. It is the state of deep satisfaction and that happiness which arises when thinking, feeling, and acting are in harmony – not as a fleeting moment, but as a steady state of being.

No Rigid Dogmas

Break away from fixed methods. Only thus do you open the gate to truth. Words are not enough. You must live it. Practice leads into "Nothingness" – that emptiness where pure consciousness awaits.

Whoever walks their path truthfully encounters universal truth.

The Unity of Opposites

Opposites are not enemies, but a unity. Measurable science unites with spiritual depth. Western strategy meets Eastern stillness. We need balance: stoic calm and unconditional compassion. We need sharpness: pragmatism, strategy, and vigilance for freedom. Non-violent power unites with pure mindfulness. Material success and spiritual greatness are not a contradiction. Everything flows together.

Let yourself be inspired. But always feel into your innermost self. Do not walk another's path. Find and walk your own, unmistakable path.

Chapter 1: Connection and Healing

Definition: Unity of Body, Mind, and Heart

True connection begins within you. Whoever is deeply rooted recognizes intuitively what strengthens and what weakens them. After phases of intense mental activity, the inner self seeks space and stillness. This space can arise in manifold ways: in nature, while running, through physical training, gardening, music, dance, art, and much more. The goal is to gain distance from the flood of thoughts. Whoever listens mindfully to the resonance of their inner self transforms self-love into active action. The healing of the individual is always the starting point of collective healing.

1.1 The Core Teaching: We Are Always Connected

We are always connected with one another. Every feeling of separation is merely an illusion of the ego, which becomes the root of fear and scarcity. Mindlessness scatters our energy into the pursuit of validation and possession – a flight from the self. The crucial question is: Do we act from the ego or from our true nature? Success may be measurable, but intention counts. The answer requires a focus inward. For only inner harmony creates true freedom.

The Ongoing Process

Holistic health arises in the harmonious interplay of three components:

  • The body is the physical foundation. Vitality through targeted training and conscious nutrition forms the indispensable basis.
  • The mind acts as the silent observer. Mindfulness dissolves identification with thoughts, which are merely passing clouds.
  • The heart is the emotional center. It allows love and gratitude to become active energy – the bridge to the outer world.

    • This is not a one-time act, but a continuous attitude. It is the consistent, daily practice of mindfulness.

    The Three Activators

    True connection is not a passive state, but active action. Three fundamental pillars support this process:

    • Conscious breathing is the anchor in the here and now. Every action occurs with full presence.
    • Bodywork offers direct access to the interior. Targeted training is a moving meditation that harmonizes the energy flow.
    • Spiritual practice is the expansion of consciousness. It unfolds, for example, in meditation, through reflection, and the experience of nature.

    The Expansion: The Greater Whole

    Inner healing opens the gate to universal existence. Fundamental principles become experienceable:

    • Nature teaches the eternal cycle.
    • The Dao reveals the flow and effortless action.
    • Zen shows the pure moment.
    • Qi connects us energetically.
    • The Logos provides structure – universal reason.

    Recognize the truth: You are connected. You are an inseparable part of the Greater Whole.

    1.2 Energy & Truth: Beyond the Ego

    The Fundamental Unity

    Ancient wisdom teaches a fundamental insight: Behind all that is visible works a shared, moving force. This deeper level of reality is inseparable. Our experienced separation therefore does not result from reality itself, but is a filter of the ego.

    Healing begins with the courage of truth. Two levels are inextricably linked:

    • Personal truth is the honest acknowledgment of the current state (wounds, successes, shadow sides) without judgment.
    • Universal truth is the eternal principle that behind every experience lie unlimited potential and order.

    Healing arises in this synthesis: We accept the imperfect present completely and recognize at the same time that it does not define us. In this confluence, we assume full responsibility.

    Beyond the Ego

    Resistance is the ego. A construct of roles and memories living in past and future. It creates separation. True healing lies beyond these boundaries. The method is not struggle, but observation: Withdraw leadership from illusion. Pure presence dissolves identification. Actions then spring from the core of the inner nature's essence.

    Tools for Liberation

    Two routines dissolve the illusion of separation:

    • Mindful observation. Recognize thoughts as passing phenomena. The guiding principle: “I have this thought, but I am not this thought.”
    • The anchor (breath). Centers you in the here and now.

    In this absolute presence, the false self finds no hold and dissolves.

    Experiencing Pure Consciousness

    In stillness, the origin reveals itself: Pure consciousness. Blockages dissolve, and we experience limitlessness. It is the courage to release limitation into universal truth.

    1.3 Holistic Healing

    The Essence of Healing

    True healing is holism – the unity of body, mind, and heart. This requires no waiting for miracles, but active discipline. Repetition is the mother of skill. Routines are not restrictive duties, but the anchor points of freedom. Discipline is the highest form of self-care. Without consistent application, the deepest insight remains pure theory.

    The Pillars of Practice

    The entire system rests on harmony. Clear pillars interlock:

    • Energy & Stillness (Qigong, yoga, meditation) clear the mind and open inner space.
    • Body & Strength (Running, calisthenics, animal moves) promote coordination and vitality.
    • Purification & Nature (Fasting, conscious nutrition, nature) support regeneration.
    • Resilience & Expression (Cold exposure, sauna, music, dance) strengthen resilience and release emotions.

    The Power of Consistency

    The secret is constancy. Daily repetition shapes character and transforms realization into reality. Thus a chain reaction arises: Each conscious action strengthens the next. An unshakeable foundation grows. Healing and growth remain in flow.

    Individuality

    Choose with clarity. Do not adopt methods blindly, but find tools that resonate with your own nature.

    1.4 Space for Development: The Power of the Pause

    The Necessity of Relief

    We often confuse growth with constant activity and the compulsion to accumulate more and more. This is unnatural and leads to exhaustion. True development requires space, which only arises through the conscious pause. Nature provides the principle: A full sponge absorbs nothing more. Overused soil loses nutrients. Body and Geist need relief for regeneration, clarity, and inspiration.

    Physical Pauses: Fasting

    Fasting is not deprivation, but an act of self-love. It is the conscious decision for recovery.

    • Cellular cleansing (Autophagy): Damaged cells are recycled – a process of renewal.
    • Relief: The gastrointestinal tract (the "second brain") receives rest.
    • Mindfulness: Conscious renunciation strengthens discipline. Impulses are observed, not blindly followed. This is a crucial step toward inner sovereignty.

    Mental Pauses: Mental Sovereignty

    We live in an era of permanent bombardment. Unfiltered information leads to overload and distraction. The solution is mental sovereignty. Conscious switching off creates immediate space. Break accessibility and give the nervous system rest. Stillness: Only when outside noise fades does inner noise settle. Stillness is not a deficiency, but the prerequisite for clarity.

    The Fertility of Emptiness

    Pauses are active creation. Pauses are not passive idleness. They are conscious design. Here the power of emptiness works, in which the essential takes place.

    • Integration: The mind processes what was experienced. Raw knowledge matures into lived wisdom only in stillness.
    • Inspiration: New things arise not under pressure, but in the intermediate spaces. An empty mind is an open vessel.
    • Inner Strength: True strength proves itself not only in forward drive. It shows in the stoic ability to withdraw from the vortex of the external into the unshakeable world of the interior.

    1.5 Action – Substance – Heaven & Earth: The Unity of Mindfulness

    True mindfulness is a dynamic balance of three inseparable pillars: Action encompasses all deeds (conscious, unconscious, active, or passive); Substance is everything we take in (oxygen, food, thoughts, and information); and Heaven & Earth form the resonance framework (places, people, environment, analog/digital world, and the time dimension, with the goal of presence).

    • ACTION: The breath is the anchor; it regulates energy and calms the system. Thinking is pure observation – without identification or entanglement. Action occurs in full presence; the quality of the deed determines the energy flowing into the world.
    • SUBSTANCE: What nourishes us shapes our state. Air, water, and food are sources of pure energy. Choose mental nourishment wisely. Focus, stillness, and inspiring thoughts are genuine sources of power – they deeply nourish and create lasting clarity.
    • HEAVEN & EARTH: This is the resonance room of existence. Space (environment & nature) shapes energy; nurture connections, set boundaries. Time means being in the here and now. Do not waste energy in past or future. Digitality is only a tool, a simulation; it must never replace the real. The analog world is the foundation.

    The Unity

    Holisticity arises in the triad. Center action through breath. Purify substance through conscious choice. Choose the framework through nature. Only in this balance can universal energy flow unhindered. Deepest healing becomes possible.

    Chapter 2: Education and Growth

    The Essence of Education

    Education is not the passive storing of facts. The Latin educo literally means „to lead forth“. True learning is an active unfolding process. It does not fill vessels; it purposefully fosters the potential that already exists.

    2.1 The Foundation

    Prosperity arises through value creation. A trained mind is the highest capital.

    • Health: Knowledge returns self-determination over the body.
    • Freedom: An enlightened person sees through dogmas and thinks autonomously.
    • Character: Education goes beyond knowledge. It shapes the values for the growth of the individual and society.

    Values and Virtues

    Potential is tied to values. Education without character remains empty and dangerous. The foundation must be ethical.

    • Integrity is the unity of thinking, speaking, and acting; fidelity to principles even against resistance.
    • Compassion is the view beyond the ego.
    • Self-reflection is the constant readiness to question oneself; the origin of all wisdom.
    • Resilience & Discipline mean that setbacks are not failure, but lessons; these virtues are the anchor in the storm.

    Education as Lived Presence

    Knowledge turns into wisdom only through experience. Education must leave theory and become deed.

    • Application: Learning happens through action. Reading is not enough. Only daily practice makes education alive.
    • Curiosity: True education answers no questions, it kindles new ones and awakens wonder at the laws of the universe.
    • Inspiration: The study of great personalities transmits a spirit. Greatness is achievable for anyone who walks the path of truth.

    Education is the process that brings the inner outward. It shapes a culture of conscious togetherness.

    2.2 The Five Pillars of Growth

    Education unfolds potential. Yet this force must be channeled so that manifest success arises. This process follows a clear map which we consciously steer through five central pillars:

    • Philosophy: The inner map. Growth does not depend on the wind (circumstances), but on the set of the sail. External events are neutral; only philosophy decides: obstacle or lesson? Whoever takes full responsibility gains the power to change.
    • Attitude: The emotional filter – felt philosophy. It acts like a thermostat. We do not control events, but our reaction to them. A growth-oriented attitude springs from trust, not fear. It charges every action with the right energy.
    • Activity: Execution. Knowledge is only potential power; activity makes it real. This is the bridge between inner and outer world – the decisive step from knowing to doing.
    • Results: The neutral truth. Results do not judge, they indicate. They are the incorruptible mirror of philosophy, attitude, and activity. View them as data points: Is quality right? Is quantity right? Use results as feedback for course correction.
    • Lifestyle: Conscious design. The synthesis of all steps. Lifestyle is no accident, it is design. Consciously create an environment and routines that foster growth. Positive results flow back and refine philosophy.

    2.3 Life is Like the Seasons

    Life does not proceed linearly. It mirrors nature. Utilize the cycles.

    • Winter: Challenges test us. We become wiser, stronger, and better. We not only survive, we grow inwardly.
    • Spring: Seize the opportunity. Be courageous and determined. Spring does not last forever.
    • Summer: Nurture the positive. Protect it. Set boundaries and weed out negativity.
    • Autumn: Take responsibility for the harvest. Reflect. Appreciate the result without excuse and without complaint.

    2.4 Personal Development: The Three Temples

    True growth synchronizes all levels.

    • The Temple of the Body (Physique): The vessel of life and instrument of action. Treat it as sacred. Without health, no potential works.
    • The Temple of the Mind (Character): The center of values. Cultivate virtues. Honesty and compassion strengthen the inner axis. A strong character remains centered in the storm.
    • The Temple of the Intellect: The seat of logic. Develop it through reflection. Question critically. Distinguish truth from illusion.

    2.5 Communication: The Power of Words

    Communication is the primary tool of manifestation. Words do not merely describe; they are energy. They can heal or hurt.

    Conscious speech is an act of creation.

    • Value (Mindfulness): Treat words like nourishment. Choose what clarifies and inspires. Effective communication begins with listening.
    • Source (Authenticity): Impact needs no rhetoric, but sincerity. Play no role. Express yourself honestly.
    • Goal (Trust): Words must be backed by deeds. When word and action match, integrity arises.

    Through language we shape the world. Communication is the ultimate test of inner growth.

    Chapter 3: Practice and Manifestation

    3.1 The Foundation: Responsibility and the First Step

    Philosophy without deed is intellectual play. Insights remain worthless as long as they are not translated into action. The foundation of execution rests on two pillars: inner attitude (responsibility) and outer movement (the first step). Here is where it is decided: manifestation or inertia.

    The Inner Attitude: Unshakeable Responsibility

    The basis is responsibility.

    True power lies only in what we control: judgments and decisions. The outside is unreliable. True change begins with full responsibility. There is no victim mentality, only creator power.

    The Dichotomy of Control

    Clearly distinguish between two areas:

    • What you control (Inner): Your action, your attitude, your values – invest 100% of your energy here.
    • What you do not control (Outer): Results, actions of others, past – waste no energy here.

    Whoever lives responsibility asks not „Why?“ upon setbacks, but: „What do I learn from this?“ Become from consumer to creator.

    Outer Movement: The First Step

    Responsibility as a concept is not enough. Practice demands physical execution. The enemy is inertia, which often masquerades as perfectionism – waiting for the ideal moment is a trap.

    The Radically Simple

    The solution lies in the small. Ask yourself: Which step have I neglected? No revolution is needed. A conscious apple (substance) or a short walk (action) suffices. The first step is the most important. It breaks the standstill and sets potential in motion.

    The Mechanism of Kinetics

    Every action acts back on the system. It strengthens the cycle.

    • Self-confidence is the signal to the mind: „I do what I say“; this builds integrity.
    • Realization arises through action; results are neutral feedback for the next cycle.
    • Momentum arises when overcoming the resistance of the beginning; whoever dares the first step breaks the barrier, and statics turns into dynamics.

    Continuous repetition of the first step is the method. Consciously steer your life in the desired direction.

    3.2 The Basis: The Anchor

    Practice is fundamental. If responsibility is the foundation, breath is the energy source. It stabilizes attitude and steers action. If I had to choose a single practice as the most important: It would be conscious breathing.

    Breath as a Universal Bridge

    Breath is the immediate proof of connectedness. It regulates the state instantly and creates connection on two levels: Physical (inner/nervous system with outer) and Mental (unconscious and conscious action). Through its control we assume responsibility. It is the anchor in the here and now.

    In moments of stress – when the ego wants to take leadership – use the breath as a lever. It brings you back to clarity.

    The Role of Breathing in the System

    • In everyday life: Deep breathing calms the system and clears the mind. Stress transforms into concentrated energy.
    • In training: Breath is fuel. It directly steers body performance and regeneration.
    • Energetically: It is the gateway to life force (Qi/Prana).

    Qigong: Mastery of Energy

    Qigong is the completion of this principle – the „work with life energy“. It is the fusion of movement and breath (the unity of action, substance, and heaven & earth). This practice harmonizes the system.

    3.3 The Body: Central Points of Practice

    Everything is connected. Everything acts on everything, nothing exists in isolation. Yet in practice, three areas stand out for me as physical anchor points. They define grounding, flexibility, and control. The key lies not in hours of training, but in the integration of continuous routines.

    • FEET: GROUNDING AND FOUNDATION. Feet are roots and resonance conductors. Strong, flexible feet are the basis for posture and injury prevention.
      The practice: Walk and run barefoot as often as possible to activate sensory perception. Whether at home or in nature. Connect with the ground. Use „barefoot shoes“ or shoes that give space instead of constraining.
    • HIP: FLEXIBILITY AND FREEDOM. The hip is the hinge of movement. Stiffness leads to pain in many areas and increases injury risk. Flexibility means physical freedom and is the key to force transmission.
      The practice: The deep squat. It is daily maintenance. Linger in this position again and again to preserve natural function.
    • BELLY: THE SECOND BRAIN. The belly is crucial. It is not just digestion, it is the enteric nervous system (ENS). A burdened belly fogs the mind.
      The practice: Grant the belly relief. Utilize fasting and pay attention to substance quality.

    CONCLUSION: Start small. Go barefoot. Squat down and give the belly rest. Integrate these micro-routines until they become part of your nature.

    3.4 The Mind: Observer and Steersman

    The mind is inseparably connected with the body. Unrest in the mind creates tension in the body. Clarity enables precise action. Practice unites two roles:

    • THE OBSERVER: Step back. Perceive thoughts without judging. Do not identify. Say: „There is anger“, not „I am angry“. Thus you create space between stimulus and response.
    • THE STEERSMAN: When space is open, assume leadership. Direct focus onto the target. Choose the action matching your values. Be no plaything of impulses. Be sovereign.

    The Essence of Presence

    The highest discipline is: „Wherever you are, be there totally.“ When mind, body, and heart are bundled, mindfulness arises. Practice „non-thinking“ in nature or linger in meditation. Conduct mental hygiene through offline times. Only a rested mind can observe clearly and steer wisely.

    3.5 Nothingness: Freedom Beyond Rules

    The Stages of Mastery

    Routines (e.g., fasting, breathwork, physical training) are the foundation. They serve to shape character until attitude (discipline) no longer needs to be forced, but arises organically. True freedom lies not in abandoning form, but in mastering form until it becomes invisible. The path leads via connection, growth, and practice. Yet the goal is not the rule. The goal is liberation from all systems. The peak of practice is letting go of practice. The return to emptiness.

    The Mastery of Nothingness

    True strategy has no form.

    Emptiness is no deficiency. It is unlimited potential. Pure consciousness, free of boundaries.

    The Danger of Dogma

    The path is paradoxical: It leads through discipline to freedom. Here lies the trap: The ego turns practices into cages. The thought „I must“ is no freedom. Tools of liberation must never become new chains.

    Growing Beyond What Was Learned

    Move freely. As with the raft in Buddhism: We use routines to cross the river. Yet on the other shore we leave the raft behind. Be like water: Adaptable. Limitless. Intuition (Wu Wei) replaces effort.

    Ultimate Freedom

    There is no longer a rule behind which to hide. Every act springs from the inner axis.

    Do not let yourself be blinded. Choose the fruit. Choose substance. Be free. Be truthful. Be yourself.

    Epilogue: The Frequency of Abundance

    Gratitude as Creation

    It is no fleeting feeling, but an active source. It is the frequency that attracts abundance and dissolves scarcity. The law of resonance shifts focus: Away from what is lacking, toward what is. This attitude shapes reality. An open heart receives. It lifts beyond the ego. Connection with the Greater Whole becomes tangible.

    May this manifesto be the spark for your inner fire.
